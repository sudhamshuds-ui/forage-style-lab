# Forage plating notes (from public X, plus art/UI references)

Collected 27 November 2026 (Australia/Sydney) from public X posts, then turned into modelable rules in our own words. Queries covered toast and open sandwiches, rice bowls (including jollof, poke, donburi, bibimbap), plated mains with purée or mash, plus shorter passes on sundaes, cakes, sushi/gimbap and burgers. Searches favoured posts with media, English text, and (where the API allowed) older dates and some engagement. Restaurant, café and chef accounts were preferred over generic promo.

**Honesty about the source.** X is uneven for this. The most useful posts were restaurant menus, home-cook plated dinners, and a few chef or catering plates. A lot of hits were low-engagement, repeated captions, course-spam, or off-topic. Michelin-level “how I plate” threads were scarce. Notes below only use posts actually retrieved; if a family was thin, that is said plainly. Links are `https://x.com/<user>/status/<id>`.

## Shared composition rules (from plating-tips posts)

These showed up across plates, not just one family:

- Treat the plate like a picture: symmetry or the rule of thirds, plus **negative space**. Empty china matters as much as the food. Don’t fill the rim.
- Use **complementary colour** and some height (a garnish, an egg, a quenelle) so the plate has more than one layer.
- Odd numbers read better than even (a commenter correcting a plate to three items).
- Keep sauce under control: a smear, a glaze on the plate, or a squeeze-bottle line — not a puddle. Fresh garnish, not frozen.

Sources: [yikesies, Jan 2019](https://x.com/yikesies/status/1082692112104206338) (symmetry, rule of thirds, negative space); [idontwan2know, Jan 2022](https://x.com/idontwan2know/status/1483142223504027648) (composition, colour, depth); [julie_at_law, Sep 2026](https://x.com/julie_at_law/status/2103669479700041987) (empty space matters); [vandana_bhalla, Feb 2025](https://x.com/vandana_bhalla/status/1886916347088478496) (colour, negative space, dimension); [eastvalleyrich, Mar 2023](https://x.com/eastvalleyrich/status/1638813582111690753) (odd number / thirds).

## 1. Toast, open sandwiches, bruschetta

X was reasonably useful here: cafés and brunches describe the same build over and over.

### Cuts and forms

- **Bread** is toasted sourdough, rustic country bread or baguette, sized to a small board or side plate — not a lonely slice on a dinner plate.
- **Avocado** is smashed or mashed on the toast (salt, pepper, often flaky salt), never a raw half sitting on the bread.
- **Tomato** is sliced, or cherry tomatoes halved, roasted or blistered. For classic bruschetta the cut is a small dice mixed with basil, oil and salt, not big wedges standing on edge.
- **Salmon** is smoked slices, flakes, or a lox layer — not a thick raw fillet.
- **Egg**, when present, is poached, sunny-side or soft scrambled, sitting on the spread, not beside it as a second meal.
- Other flat toppings seen often: pickled red onion, radish, cucumber, thin bacon or streaky, crumbled or soft cheese, pea shoots or arugula.

### Layer order

1. Warm toasted bread (for bruschetta, rub a garlic clove on the cut face while it is still hot).
2. A spread first: smashed avocado, cream cheese, whipped ricotta, pesto or tapenade.
3. Protein and sliced toppings laid **flat and slightly overlapping** (tomato slices, salmon, cucumber, onion).
4. A finish: herbs or pea shoots, flaky salt, cracked pepper, everything-bagel seasoning, a chilli sauce, lemon, or roe/caviar on a special tartine.

### Height, colour, portion

- The stack stays fairly flat so it still reads as toast. Height comes from an egg, a few shoots, or blistered tomatoes, not from chunks piled into a mound.
- Colour contrast is built in: green smash, red tomato, pink salmon, pale onion, dark bread, a herb or shoot on top.
- One or two slices is the usual portion. A restaurant tartine may be more loaded, but still open-faced and layered, not a closed sandwich.

### Sources

- [Block 16, Jul 2026](https://x.com/Block16Omaha/status/2072342583771099234) — open salmon tartine: whipped spread, flaked hot-smoked salmon, arugula, pickled onion, blistered cherry tomatoes, cucumber and roe on house sourdough.
- [15 Minute Recipes, Nov 2026](https://x.com/15mr_app/status/2124775627114893402) — bruschetta: rub garlic on warm toast; baguette, Roma tomatoes, basil, oil, balsamic.

## 2. Rice bowls (jollof, poke, donburi, bibimbap)

Stronger than toast for **jollof and bibimbap**; poke and donburi showed up as ingredient lists more than plating essays.

### Jollof and mixed West African plates

- The rice itself should look **colourful and “complete”** — cooks said jollof is not done if it doesn’t have a mouth-watering look filled with vegetables. Flecks of pepper, tomato and onion in the rice matter; a single microherb on plain orange rice is not enough.
- A chef plating study put on one plate: **moi-moi, jollof, chicken, salad, and spring onion as the garnish**. That is a composed plate with several colours and a green finish, not rice alone.
- Home and catering plates almost always add **golden fried plantain (dodo)**, a salad or slaw, and a cooked protein (chicken, turkey or fish). The protein is often a **whole piece set beside the rice**, not a giant raw-looking drumstick jammed into the bowl. If the meat goes *in* the rice, it should be cut (sliced, diced or shredded) and partly sunk, with browned skin or glaze.

### Donburi, poke, Korean bowls

- **Katsudon:** a golden panko cutlet served over soft egg and rice — the cutlet is the hero, the egg is a soft bed, not a hard-boiled garnish on the rim.
- **Poke / mixed bowls:** rice as the base; protein in bite-size pieces; then a ring or mosaic of fresh and pickled vegetables (cucumber, avocado, edamame, pineapple, purple cabbage, seaweed salad), nori, a drizzle (teriyaki or mayo), and a bright finish such as roe. Colour blocks sit next to each other, not in one muddy pile (until the diner mixes).
- **Beef or glazed bowls:** a savory-sweet glaze on sliced or minced meat, **sesame seeds and spring onion** on top. Some cooks try for a **rice dome** in the bowl before the topping goes on.
- **Bibimbap:** rice, then a **rainbow of sautéed vegetables** arranged in distinct colour groups, an egg (poached or fried) and a spoon of gochujang. Sesame oil — or, in one dramatised version, browned butter — goes over, and the dish is meant to be mixed. The unmixed bowl should already look colourful.

### Height vs flatness, portion

- Bowls can have a little height (a cutlet, an egg, a dome of rice) but toppings should sit in or on the rice, not hover. Leave a ring of bowl visible.
- Garnish is mixed colour, not one herb: spring onion, sesame, pickle, chilli, plantain, salad leaves, roe.

### Sources

- [Le Japhé, Nov 2026](https://x.com/officialLeJaphe/status/2118231726132457713) — pastry/cuisine chef, plating practice: moi-moi, jollof, chicken, salad, spring onion garnish.
- [princess_ehmy, Nov 2026](https://x.com/princess_ehmy/status/2125454660215402900) — jollof should have a colourful, vegetable-filled look.
- [Rufas, Nov 2026](https://x.com/Rufas783/status/2123658130303160655) — katsudon: panko pork cutlet over soft egg and rice.
- [K for All, Nov 2026](https://x.com/kforall_/status/2125923271980077557) — bibimbap build: rice and sautéed vegetables, poached egg, gochujang, fat (brown butter or sesame oil), then mix.
- [hcunwook, Nov 2026](https://x.com/hcunwook/status/2125031301812281839) — beef rice bowl with savory-sweet glaze, sesame and green onion.

Also consistent with: [csstupid, Nov 2026](https://x.com/csstupid/status/2123590180926062847) (salmon poke with pickled veg, avocado, edamame, cabbage, seaweed, mayo, roe); [K-VERSE, Nov 2026](https://x.com/KVERSE_official/status/2123981800070697339) (Jeonju bibimbap as a “rainbow” bowl); [ezemor4568, Nov 2026](https://x.com/ezemor4568/status/2125126131988791435) (smoky jollof with chicken and golden dodo); [JEdK84, Nov 2026](https://x.com/JEdK84/status/2125421532943876151) (Korean beef bowl, sauce, attempt at a rice dome).

## 3. Plated mains with purée or mash, and sliced meat

This family had the clearest *technique* language, even when engagement was modest.

### How purée and mash are served

- **Never a large smooth dome parked on the side.** Two forms showed up again and again:
  - A **back-of-the-spoon smear** (or “purée smear”) of the purée **on the plate**, as a painted base or swoosh, with the meat sitting on or against it.
  - A **quenelle** — a small, tight, football-shaped scoop of potato or flavoured purée — as a deliberate shape beside the protein, not a blob.
- One home cook, looking at their own plate, said they should have put the smear **on the plate instead of on the steak**. Purée is a bed or a side shape, not icing on the meat.
- Jus, glaze or Bordelaise is also **on the plate** (a pool or stroke under or around the steak), not a flood. If sauce is piped, it needs enough body to hold a line (too-runny sauce was called out as a plating failure).

### How meat is cut and placed

- Steak and fillet are **seared**, often described as medium-rare, sometimes with clear sear marks. Colour is a browned crust with a pink centre, not an even ham-pink cube.
- Plated examples sit the meat on or against the smear or glaze, with sides (asparagus, charred broccolini, confit shallot, mushroom, roasted vegetables) tucked around, and **microherbs or vegetable petals** as the last colour.
- A fuller “restaurant” plate from one home cook: steak, fondant potato, asparagus, a beetroot purée, a **quenelle** of a rich emulsion, and a mushroom jus — several small, distinct elements, not one heap.
- Beef cheek or similar slow meat is still plated with a creamy potato, a glossy jus, roasted vegetables and microherbs — cooked colour, not raw.

### Portion and space

- Protein is a steak, a couple of medallions, or a tidy slice group — not a towering stack. Leave negative space on a wide plate.
- The quenelle is small relative to the meat. The smear is a gesture, not a lagoon.

### Sources

- [Stephen Sawrey, Dec 2020](https://x.com/scs1304/status/1334578728723214342) — names the “back of the spoon smear on the plate” (and a piping bag) as the standard purée presentation.
- [Paul Pichugin, Aug 2026](https://x.com/paulmp/status/2086045397726994668) — wagyu with fondant potato, asparagus, beetroot purée, a quenelle of black-garlic and marrow emulsion, and red-wine mushroom jus.
- [Simon Lacey, Feb 2021](https://x.com/Laceyscheese/status/1361011467814854656) — sous-vide ribeye, red-wine sauce, confit shallot and garlic, mushroom fricassee, **quenelle of parsley-pomme purée**.
- [Anthony Jestes, Dec 2020](https://x.com/Antog1331/status/1335676160513019904) — merlot glaze on the plate, medium-rare fillet, golden potato-purée quenelle, roasted Brussels petals.
- [TheOne's Kitchen, Apr 2024](https://x.com/TheOnesKitchen/status/1781694988889334174) — fillet with a shishito-mushroom purée; the cook notes the smear should have been on the plate, not on the steak.

Also: [imduffy15, Aug 2021](https://x.com/imduffy15/status/1422214856514711552) (steak with hatched sear lines and a purée smear); [xingtian991314, Jul 2025](https://x.com/xingtian991314/status/1945104087575634076) (beef cheek, creamy saffron potato, glossy jus, roasted vegetables, microherbs); [Town & Country Fine Foods, Jun 2018](https://x.com/tcfinefoods/status/1006527507389087750) (a dessert quenelle with fruit purée — same shape language).

## 4. Later families (short; X was uneven)

### Burgers

Useful, but mostly restaurant captions, not stylist essays. The pattern is consistent enough to model:

- **Toasted bun** (brioche is the default; sometimes potato or sesame). Often buttered.
- Patties are **smash-style with a crisp browned edge**, often a double stack of small patties (around 3–4 oz each) with melted cheese between or on them.
- Flat layers, in order, something like: heel, sauce, shredded lettuce, tomato slice, onion, pickle, patty, cheese, bun. Shredded lettuce and thin tomato slices show up more than whole leaves or thick wedges.
- The burger is a **tight stack**, messy in a good way, not ingredients scattered on the plate. Fries sit beside it.

Sources: [Barnacle, Jun 2025](https://x.com/Barnaclelpool/status/1931032151593554139); [Delburne Bistro, Apr 2025](https://x.com/DelburneBistro/status/1909976817760718968); [JAM Eateries, Jul 2025](https://x.com/JAMEateries/status/1942249137178579152); [Coast Packing Co., Sep 2025](https://x.com/CoastPackingCo/status/1968793405842145707).

### Sundaes

Lots of repeated “classic sundae” language, almost no chef plating. Treat this as a folk consensus, not a stylist source:

- Two or three scoops in a cup, boat or tall glass.
- Warm sauce (hot fudge, caramel, sometimes marshmallow) draped over the scoops.
- Whipped cream, then nuts or sprinkles, with a **cherry on top**.
- Banana split: bananas split lengthwise as the base, several flavours of scoop, sauces and nuts.

Sources: [zah78177, May 2025](https://x.com/zah78177/status/1928360984042951003); [crow___boy, May 2021](https://x.com/crow___boy/status/1395554353566605312). Engagement on these was low.

## References for art direction and UI

Searched X for painterly 3D food, Three.js/WebGL food, and cozy cooking games. That pass was **not useful**: it mostly returned PlayStation Plus catalogue announcements that merely listed *Dave the Diver* and *Nour: Play With Your Food*, with no art-direction discussion. The references below are from the open web. Each link was HTTP-checked (200) on 27 November 2026. An ArtStation cooking-UI case study was skipped after it returned 403.

- **Sebastiano** — [live site](https://sebastiano.polyarix.space/), [case study](https://polyarix.com/works/sebastiano). Three.js restaurant story-site. Soft toy-like food forms, a tight colour system shared by UI and 3D, flat poster-like light, and ink outlines. Borrow: one world for the dish and the chrome around it; outlines that thin on a small screen.
- **Loaf (Baker Games)** — [devlog](https://baker.games/devlog/meet-loaf). Procedural Three.js bread with a painted toon shader: one soft shadow edge, warm (not grey) shadow, bake-noise in the crust, a hard glaze highlight, inverted-hull outline. Borrow: food should read as painted, not as a product photo; silhouette first.
- **Jelly Painting (mesq)** — [live demo](https://mesq.me/jelly-painting/), [80.lv write-up](https://80.lv/articles/look-at-this-jiggly-jelly-dessert-made-with-three-js). Stylized Three.js dessert: hand-painted normals, brushstroke colour, fixed dither, soft-body jiggle, glass-like transmission. Borrow: stroke and material work on close-up food; drinks and jelly need real contact with the glass.
- **Pasta — a study in shape (Ruben van de Ven)** — [live experiment](https://rubenv.net/experiments/pasta). Three.js pasta plates you can twirl, rake and watch sauce drip, then “develop” into a photograph. Borrow: sauce as a viscous coating that gathers and drips; interaction that invites a close look at the food.
- **Sizzle & Chaos** — [GitHub](https://github.com/tuxin-openclaw/sizzle-and-chaos). Overcooked-like Three.js / R3F kitchen: cut, cook, plate, steam and flame. Borrow: kitchen loop and particle cues, not the low-poly party look.
- **Brushfield / WebGL Style Library** — [live playground](https://webgl-style-library.vercel.app/). Painterly post-process (Kuwahara strokes, Sobel outlines, posterize, paper grain) on a Three.js scene, with tunables. Borrow: a brush-size control next to the painter styles, which matches Forage’s planned slider.
- **Watercolor 3D (tommyato)** — [live lab](https://tommyato.com/labs/watercolor-3d/). Watercolour post-process: edge bleed, paper texture, hand-drawn wobble. Borrow: only if Forage ever wants a wetter, paper-like style beside the oil-paint looks.
- **Yuasa Yuu, sushi still life** — [80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/sushi-3d-scene-with-painterly-texture-crafted-with-blender). Hand-painted / shader-painted 3D sushi that reads as watercolour. Borrow: painted texture on rice, fish and garnish rather than photo textures.
- **Venba** — [PlayStation Blog interview](https://blog.playstation.com/2023/06/12/the-story-of-venbas-culinary-and-cultural-roots-from-creative-director-abhi/), [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1868140/VENBA/). Soft, stylized food that stays authentic in shape and colour (the team cooked the dishes first). Borrow: stylize without losing what makes the dish recognisable.
- **Dave the Diver** — [Game Developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/dave-the-diver), [art-direction notes](https://teemo.dev/game-design/dave-the-diver/systems/art-direction-and-visual-language/). Dish art keeps each fish’s iconic feature on the plate (angler sushi keeps the lantern). Borrow: the ingredient should still be readable after it is cooked and plated.
- **Nour: Play With Your Food** — [official site](https://food.game/), [Game Developer interview](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/tying-music-and-food-aesthetics-together-for-an-oddly-satisfying-experience-with-nour-play-with-your-food), [Dropbox interview](https://blog.dropbox.com/topics/work-culture/nour-game-play-with-your-food-3d-artist-tj-hughes). High-saturation, close-up 3D food meant to make you hungry (anime and commercial food styling as references; shaders for tea, noodles, gloss). Borrow: appetite as the art test; material care on sauces, ice cream and drinks — without copying the neon pop-art look, which is further from Forage’s painterly kitchen.
- **Amayadori** — [press kit](https://sol3games.com/amayadori/press/). Cozy ramen game with painterly lo-fi art and no timers; bowls are built step by step (tare, broth, noodles, negi, chashu, egg) and a careful layout is rewarded. Borrow: calm kitchen pacing; a bowl that is assembled in real layers.
- **Kokoro Kitchen** — [review](https://playcritik.com/en/review/kokoro-kitchen). Soft, warm food illustration and a restful palette. Borrow: tone of the finished-plate pride, and a UI that doesn’t shout over the food.
- **Pizza Slice (Underdraw Studio)** — [UI case study](https://underdraw.studio/work/pizza-slice/). Menus moved onto a kitchen order slip so the UI feels like part of the room. Borrow: hide tool-y chrome (Forage’s test search already does this); let panels feel like kitchen objects when they must show.
