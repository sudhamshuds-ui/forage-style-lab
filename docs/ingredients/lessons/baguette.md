# Baguette

Sources: session "3D ingredient forms" (2026-11-04), commits d913ad5..1e0a285.

## Colour
- **Baked colour is warm and varied, never one tan** (baguette).
  User said: "need more texture colours" and "add some more orange/reds hues to teh bread".
  Ended on: the warmer `baked` family, a toastier top, paler sides, blotches, pores and flour specks; crumb with holes, streaks and a toasted rim, never flat white.
  Why: a crust is many browns; one tan reads as plastic.

## Composition
- **A long ingredient lies on a diagonal** (baguette).
  Claude's note: the 32 cm baguette lies on a diagonal so it fits the card and shows its length in perspective.

## Form
- **Make it the version people picture** (baguette).
  First try: a round boule with a scored cross.
  User said: "make it more like a baguette but also keep this shape in memory for future use."
  Ended on: the baguette; the boule kept as the `loaf` form for later, and a sandwich loaf later as "toast".
  Why: the card has one picture; it should be the most recognisable version.
- **Cuts are varied and spread out, with pointed tips** (baguette).
  User said: "the slashes are very uniform...and clumped up...the refs dont ahve rouneded corners".
  Ended on: four varied, spread-out slashes with pointed tips and crumb inside.
  Why: repeated, evenly spaced marks look machine-made.

## Recurring errors
- **Contact shadows follow the footprint, never one big circle** (baguette).
  Claude's note: one round contact spot poked out as a blob beside the long loaf; spots now follow each piece's footprint, a row along a long piece.
  Why: a shadow that does not match the shape reads as a separate object.
- **A part set into another follows the real surface, never an ideal one** (baguette).
  User said: "we have one artifact in the bread btw" (2026-11-09: a pale shard at a slash tip).
  Ended on: the crumb placed by the crust's real height, stopping short of each groove's tips and sinking there; rim glints off; broader, rolled slash lips on a finer mesh (no sawtooth).
  Why: the loaf is a little lopsided, so the crumb's tips poked through where the ideal shape sat higher than the real crust.
