## What it teaches

- **The reference's form comes first, even on "easy" shapes**: the signature feature (a peach's
  cleft and cheeks, a tomato's curling sepals, a garlic's slim leaning neck, a carton's peaks) has
  to be there and pushed until it reads. A plain lathe with good colour is not enough.
- **The user sees the working renders too.** They preferred an earlier pear outline they saw in
  passing. When a form changes between renders, say so and show both; never swap one quietly.
- **A group is a composition, not a scatter** (mushrooms): pieces lean on and overlap each other
  the way the reference's do; nothing stands stiffly upright or looks as if it hovers.
- **Rough painted texture carries form** (potato): without it a matte, even surface reads flat.
- **Cut flesh needs light inside it** (lime): translucency and subsurface, and a texture drawn
  from the reference, never a noise pattern that can read as a glitch.
